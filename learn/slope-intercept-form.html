Slope-Intercept Form (y = mx + b), Step by Step

Slope-intercept form is y = mx + b. The number m is the slope, and b is the y-intercept, the height where the line crosses the y-axis. Once an equation is in this form, you can graph it at a glance.

Try it with your own problem ↓

The steps

  1. To read a line: m (the number in front of x) is the slope, and b (the number added at the end) is where it crosses the y-axis.
  2. To convert another form, get y alone: move the x term to the other side by adding or subtracting.
  3. Divide every term by the number in front of y.
  4. To graph: plot (0, b), then use the slope as rise over run to find more points.
  5. To write an equation from the slope m and a point, put them in y = mx + b and solve for b.

A worked example

Write 2x + 3y = 6 in slope-intercept form.

  1. Subtract 2x from both sides: 3y = −2x + 6.
  2. Divide every term by 3: y = (−2/3)x + 2.
  3. Read it: slope m = −2/3 and y-intercept b = 2.
  4. To graph: plot (0, 2), then go down 2 and right 3 to reach (3, 0). Check: 2(3) + 3(0) = 6. ✓

y = −(2/3)x + 2.

Common mistakes

  • Dividing only one term by 3. Divide every term on the right side.
  • Mixing up m and b. m multiplies x. b is the term with no x.
  • Losing the minus sign. Subtracting 2x gives −2x, so the slope is negative.
  • Plotting the intercept on the x-axis. b is where the line crosses the y-axis, at (0, b).

Questions people ask

What is the y-intercept?

The point where the line crosses the y-axis, where x = 0. In y = mx + b it is (0, b).

How do I find the equation from two points?

Find the slope from the two points, then use one point to solve for b in y = mx + b.

What are the other forms of a line?

Standard form (Ax + By = C) and point-slope form (y − y₁ = m(x − x₁)).
