How to Take a Derivative with the Power Rule

The derivative of a function tells you its rate of change, the slope of its graph at each point. For terms like xⁿ, there is a quick shortcut called the power rule.

Try it with your own problem ↓

The steps

  1. Power rule: the derivative of xⁿ is n · xⁿ⁻¹. Bring the exponent down in front, then subtract 1 from the exponent.
  2. A number in front stays and is multiplied: the derivative of a · xⁿ is a · n · xⁿ⁻¹.
  3. The derivative of x (that is, x¹) is 1, and the derivative of any plain number is 0.
  4. For a sum or difference, take the derivative of each term separately.

A worked example

Find the derivative of f(x) = 4x³ − 5x² + 7x − 9.

  1. 4x³: bring down the 3 and multiply by 4 to get 12, and subtract 1 from the exponent: 12x².
  2. −5x²: 2 × −5 = −10, and the exponent becomes 1: −10x.
  3. 7x: the derivative of x is 1, so 7 × 1 = 7.
  4. −9: a plain number, so the derivative is 0.
  5. Put it together: f′(x) = 12x² − 10x + 7.

f′(x) = 12x² − 10x + 7.

Common mistakes

  • Forgetting to lower the exponent. The derivative of x³ is 3x², not 3x³.
  • Forgetting the coefficient. The derivative of 4x³ is 12x², not 3x².
  • Keeping the constant. A plain number has derivative 0.
  • Using the power rule on 2ˣ. That is a different kind of function. The rule is only for xⁿ.

Questions people ask

What does the derivative mean?

The slope of the tangent line at a point, or how fast a quantity is changing. If f is position, f′ is velocity.

How do I find the slope at a specific point?

Put the x-value into the derivative. For f′(x) = 12x² − 10x + 7 at x = 1: 12 − 10 + 7 = 9.
