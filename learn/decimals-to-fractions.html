How to Convert a Decimal to a Fraction

A decimal is already a fraction written another way. The digits after the decimal point are the top, and a power of 10 (10, 100, 1,000, …) is the bottom. Then you simplify.

Try it with your own problem ↓

The steps

  1. Count the digits after the decimal point.
  2. Write those digits (without the point) as the numerator.
  3. Write 1 followed by that many zeros as the denominator: one digit gives tenths, two give hundredths, three give thousandths.
  4. Simplify by dividing the top and bottom by their GCF.
  5. If there is a whole number part, keep it in front as a mixed number.

A worked example

Write 0.375 as a fraction.

  1. There are 3 digits after the decimal point, so the denominator is 1,000.
  2. The digits are 375, so the fraction is 375/1000.
  3. The GCF of 375 and 1000 is 125 (375 = 3 × 125 and 1000 = 8 × 125).
  4. Divide both by 125: 375 ÷ 125 = 3 and 1000 ÷ 125 = 8.

0.375 = 375/1000 = 3/8.

Why it works

Place value names the denominator for you. In 0.375 the 3 is in the tenths place, the 7 in the hundredths and the 5 in the thousandths, so the whole thing is 375 thousandths. Saying it aloud, "three hundred seventy-five thousandths", gives you 375/1000 straight away.

Common mistakes

  • Using the wrong power of 10. 0.375 has three decimal places, so the denominator is 1,000, not 100.
  • Dropping a zero. 0.05 is 5/100 = 1/20, not 5/10.
  • Not simplifying. 375/1000 is right, but most teachers want 3/8.
  • Forgetting the whole number. 2.45 is 2 9/20, not 9/20.

Questions people ask

How do I write a decimal with a whole number part, like 2.45?

Write the whole number, then the decimal part as a fraction: 2 and 45/100, which simplifies to 2 9/20. As an improper fraction, that is 49/20.

What if the decimal repeats?

Repeating decimals need an algebra trick: 0.333… is 1/3. Use the fractions helper below to see the steps.

What is 0.5 as a fraction?

5/10, which simplifies to 1/2.
