How to Calculate IV Drip Rate in gtt/min

A gravity IV (one without a pump) is set by counting drops in the drip chamber. The drop factor on the tubing package says how many drops (gtt) make 1 mL: 10, 15 or 20 for macrodrip sets, and 60 for microdrip.

For learning only, not for patient care. With a real patient, always follow the order, the drug’s label and your facility’s policies, and double-check every dose with a pharmacist or a second nurse.

The steps

  1. Read the volume (mL), the time and the drop factor (gtt/mL).
  2. Change the time to minutes: hours × 60.
  3. gtt/min = volume (mL) × drop factor (gtt/mL) ÷ time (min).
  4. Round to a whole drop: you can’t count part of a drop.
  5. Check with the shortcut: mL/hr ÷ (60 ÷ drop factor).

A worked example

Infuse 1,000 mL over 8 hours using tubing with a drop factor of 10 gtt/mL. Find the drip rate.

  1. Time in minutes: 8 × 60 = 480 min.
  2. Multiply the volume by the drop factor: 1,000 × 10 = 10,000 drops.
  3. Divide by the minutes: 10,000 ÷ 480 ≈ 20.83 gtt/min.
  4. Round to a whole drop: 21 gtt/min.
  5. Check: 1,000 ÷ 8 = 125 mL/hr and 60 ÷ 10 = 6, so 125 ÷ 6 ≈ 20.83. ✓

Set the roller clamp to about 21 gtt/min.

Why it works

Each mL is a fixed number of drops, so multiplying the volume by the drop factor counts every drop that has to fall: 10,000 here. Spreading those drops evenly over 480 minutes gives the drops for each minute. The units show it too: mL × gtt/mL ÷ min leaves gtt/min.

Common mistakes

  • Leaving the time in hours. The answer is drops per minute, so the time must be in minutes.
  • Using the wrong drop factor. Take it from the tubing package. With microdrip (60 gtt/mL) the answer equals the mL/hr.
  • Keeping the decimal. You count whole drops, so 20.83 becomes 21.
  • Not recounting. Count for 15 seconds and multiply by 4, then recount for a full minute.

Questions people ask

What is the drip rate for 500 mL over 4 hours with a drop factor of 15?

4 hours is 240 minutes, and 500 × 15 ÷ 240 = 31.25, so 31 gtt/min.

Why does microdrip tubing give the same number as mL/hr?

With 60 drops per mL, gtt/min = mL/hr × 60 ÷ 60. The two 60s cancel, so 50 mL/hr is 50 gtt/min.

How many drops should I see in 15 seconds?

Divide the rate by 4. For 21 gtt/min, about 5 drops in 15 seconds.
