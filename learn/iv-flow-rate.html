How to Calculate IV Flow Rate in mL/hr

An IV pump is set in milliliters per hour (mL/hr). To find the rate, divide the volume to infuse by the time it should take, in hours.

For learning only, not for patient care. With a real patient, always follow the order, the drug’s label and your facility’s policies, and double-check every dose with a pharmacist or a second nurse.

Try it with your own problem ↓

The steps

  1. Read the volume and the time. Change liters to milliliters (1 L = 1,000 mL).
  2. Put the time in hours. If it is in minutes, divide by 60 (30 min = 0.5 hr).
  3. Divide: mL/hr = volume ÷ hours.
  4. Round the way your pump is set: usually to a whole mL/hr, or to a tenth on pumps that take it.
  5. Check: rate × hours should give back about the volume.

A worked example

Infuse 1,000 mL of normal saline over 6 hours. What rate do you set on the pump?

  1. Volume 1,000 mL, time 6 hours.
  2. Divide: 1,000 ÷ 6 = 166.666… mL/hr.
  3. Round to a whole number: 167 mL/hr (166.7 on a pump that takes tenths).
  4. Check: 167 × 6 = 1,002 mL, about the 1,000 mL ordered.

Set the pump to 167 mL/hr.

Why it works

A rate is an amount for each unit of time, like miles per hour. If 1,000 mL has to go in over 6 hours, each hour gets an equal share: one sixth of 1,000 mL. Dividing the volume by the hours is sharing it out evenly.

Common mistakes

  • Leaving the time in minutes. 100 mL over 30 minutes is 200 mL/hr, not 3.3.
  • Dividing the wrong way. It is volume ÷ time. 6 ÷ 1,000 is not a rate you can set.
  • Rounding too early. Divide first, then round once at the end.
  • Mixing up mL/hr and gtt/min. Pumps use mL/hr; a gravity drip is counted in drops per minute.

Questions people ask

What is the rate for 100 mL over 30 minutes?

30 minutes is 0.5 hour, so 100 ÷ 0.5 = 200 mL/hr.

How long will 1,000 mL take at 125 mL/hr?

Time = volume ÷ rate = 1,000 ÷ 125 = 8 hours. Started at 0700, it finishes at 1500.

How do I set a rate for a dose like 2 mg/hr?

Find the concentration first: 100 mg in 100 mL is 1 mg/mL, so 2 mg/hr needs 2 mL/hr.
