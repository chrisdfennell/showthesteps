Dosage Calculations with D/H × Q (Tablets and Liquids)

The D/H × Q formula (“desired over have, times quantity”) tells you how much of a medicine to give. D is the dose ordered, H is the strength you have, and Q is the amount that strength comes in: 1 tablet, or so many mL.

For learning only, not for patient care. With a real patient, always follow the order, the drug’s label and your facility’s policies, and double-check every dose with a pharmacist or a second nurse.

Try it with your own problem ↓

The steps

  1. Read D (the order), H (the strength on the label) and Q (what H comes in).
  2. Put D and H in the same unit: 1 g = 1,000 mg and 1 mg = 1,000 mcg.
  3. Divide D by H, then multiply by Q: amount = D ÷ H × Q.
  4. Round the way you can give it: liquids to a tenth of a mL, tablets to whole or half tablets (halves only if the tablet is scored).
  5. Check: the amount × the strength should give back the dose ordered.

A worked example

The order is 375 mg. The label says 250 mg per 5 mL. How many mL do you give?

  1. D = 375 mg, H = 250 mg, Q = 5 mL. The units already match.
  2. D ÷ H = 375 ÷ 250 = 1.5.
  3. × Q: 1.5 × 5 mL = 7.5 mL.
  4. Check: 7.5 mL × 250 mg per 5 mL = 7.5 × 50 = 375 mg. ✓

Give 7.5 mL.

Why it works

D ÷ H says how many “have” portions fit into the dose you want. Here 375 mg is one and a half times 250 mg, so you need one and a half of the 5 mL portions the label describes. Multiplying by Q turns “how many portions” into an amount you can measure.

Common mistakes

  • Not matching the units. An order in g and a label in mg must be put in one unit first, or the answer is off by 1,000 times.
  • Flipping the fraction. It is desired over have: 375/250, not 250/375. A dose bigger than the strength needs more than one Q.
  • Forgetting Q. With a liquid, D ÷ H counts 5 mL portions, not mL. Multiply by Q.
  • Splitting a tablet that is not scored. Only scored tablets can be halved.

Questions people ask

How many tablets for 0.5 g if the tablets are 250 mg?

Change 0.5 g to 500 mg first. 500 ÷ 250 × 1 tablet = 2 tablets.

Can I use ratio and proportion instead?

Yes. 250 mg : 5 mL = 375 mg : x mL gives 250x = 1,875, so x = 7.5 mL. It is the same calculation.

What is dimensional analysis?

Writing the problem as a chain of fractions so the units cancel: 375 mg × 5 mL/250 mg = 7.5 mL. The mg cancel and leave mL.
