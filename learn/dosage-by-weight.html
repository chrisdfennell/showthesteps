How to Calculate a Dose by Weight (mg/kg)

Many medicines, especially for children, are ordered by weight: so many milligrams for each kilogram the patient weighs (mg/kg). The math takes two steps: get the weight in kilograms, then multiply.

For learning only, not for patient care. With a real patient, always follow the order, the drug’s label and your facility’s policies, and double-check every dose with a pharmacist or a second nurse.

Try it with your own problem ↓

The steps

  1. Get the weight in kilograms. If it is in pounds, divide by 2.2 (1 kg = 2.2 lb), and round the kilograms to a tenth.
  2. Multiply: dose = mg/kg × kg. The kilograms cancel and leave milligrams.
  3. If the order is per day (mg/kg/day) and divided, divide the daily dose by the number of doses: q8h is 3 a day, q6h is 4, q12h is 2.
  4. Compare with any maximum dose or safe range, and question an order outside it.
  5. If the drug is a liquid, turn the milligrams into milliliters with D/H × Q.

A worked example

The order is 20 mg/kg for a child who weighs 55 lb. How many milligrams is the dose?

  1. Pounds to kilograms: 55 ÷ 2.2 = 25 kg.
  2. Multiply: 20 mg/kg × 25 kg = 500 mg.
  3. Check the size: 25 is less than 55, as it should be, because a kilogram is heavier than a pound.

The dose is 500 mg (for 25 kg).

Why it works

A mg/kg order is a rate, like a price per pound at the store: the bigger the patient, the more medicine it takes, so the dose grows with the weight. Writing it as 20 mg/1 kg × 25 kg shows the kilograms cancelling and leaving milligrams, which is a good check that the problem is set up right.

Common mistakes

  • Multiplying the pounds by 2.2. That makes the weight, and the dose, almost 5 times too big. Pounds ÷ 2.2 = kilograms.
  • Using the pounds as if they were kilograms. 20 × 55 = 1,100 mg, more than twice the right dose.
  • Missing “per day”. For 40 mg/kg/day divided q8h, each dose is a third of the daily amount, not the whole amount.
  • Skipping the safe-range check. Always compare with the reference range or the maximum dose.

Questions people ask

How do I work out a mg/kg/day dose divided q8h?

For 40 mg/kg/day and 15 kg: 40 × 15 = 600 mg a day. q8h means every 8 hours, 3 doses a day, so each dose is 600 ÷ 3 = 200 mg.

Is 1 kg exactly 2.2 lb?

1 kg is about 2.2046 lb. Nursing math uses 2.2 unless your school or facility says otherwise.

How do I check a safe dose range?

Work out the low and high ends from the reference (for example 25–50 mg/kg/day), then see whether the ordered dose falls between them.
