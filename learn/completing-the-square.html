How to Solve by Completing the Square

Completing the square turns x² + bx into a perfect square like (x − 3)². Then you can solve by taking square roots. It also shows the vertex of a parabola.

Try it with your own problem ↓

The steps

  1. Make sure the x² coefficient is 1 (divide everything by it if not).
  2. Move the constant to the right side.
  3. Take half of the x coefficient, square it, and add it to both sides.
  4. Factor the left side as a perfect square: (x + half)².
  5. Take the square root of both sides (± on the right), then solve for x.

A worked example

Solve x² − 6x + 2 = 0.

  1. Move the constant: x² − 6x = −2.
  2. Half of −6 is −3, and (−3)² = 9. Add 9 to both sides: x² − 6x + 9 = −2 + 9 = 7.
  3. The left side factors: (x − 3)² = 7.
  4. Take the square root of both sides: x − 3 = ±√7.
  5. Add 3: x = 3 ± √7.

x = 3 ± √7, which is about 5.646 and 0.354.

Common mistakes

  • Adding the square to only one side. Add it to both sides to keep the equation balanced.
  • Using the full coefficient instead of half. Half of −6 is −3, not −6.
  • Forgetting the ±. A square root gives two answers.
  • Sign slip in the factor. The sign inside matches the sign of the x term: x² − 6x + 9 = (x − 3)².

Questions people ask

Why does it work?

(x − 3)² expands to x² − 6x + 9. Adding 9 gives the missing piece that makes a perfect square.

What if the x² coefficient isn't 1?

Divide the whole equation by it first. For 2x² − 8x + 3, factor 2 out of the x terms.

How does it help find a vertex?

y = a(x − h)² + k has its vertex at (h, k). For y = 2x² − 8x + 3 this gives 2(x − 2)² − 5, so the vertex is (2, −5).
