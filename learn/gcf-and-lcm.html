How to Find the GCF and LCM of Two Numbers

The greatest common factor (GCF) of two numbers is the largest number that divides both. The least common multiple (LCM) is the smallest number that both divide into. GCF is used to simplify fractions, and LCM to find common denominators.

Try it with your own problem ↓

The steps

  1. GCF: list all the factors of each number.
  2. Circle the factors that are in both lists. The biggest one is the GCF.
  3. LCM: list the first several multiples of each number.
  4. The smallest number in both lists is the LCM.
  5. Check: GCF × LCM = the first number × the second number.

A worked example

Find the GCF and LCM of 12 and 18.

  1. Factors of 12: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12.
  2. Factors of 18: 1, 2, 3, 6, 9, 18.
  3. Shared factors: 1, 2, 3, 6. The greatest is 6.
  4. Multiples of 12: 12, 24, 36, 48, …
  5. Multiples of 18: 18, 36, 54, …
  6. The first number in both lists is 36.
  7. Check: 6 × 36 = 216 and 12 × 18 = 216. ✓

GCF(12, 18) = 6 and LCM(12, 18) = 36.

Common mistakes

  • Mixing up GCF and LCM. The GCF is never bigger than either number; the LCM is never smaller.
  • Stopping the multiples too soon. Keep listing until a number appears in both lists.
  • Using the product as the LCM. 12 × 18 = 216 is a common multiple, but not the least one.
  • Missing a factor. Find factors in pairs (1 × 12, 2 × 6, 3 × 4) so none are skipped.

Questions people ask

How do I find the GCF with prime factors?

Write both prime factorizations and multiply the primes they share, the smaller power of each. 12 = 2² × 3 and 18 = 2 × 3², so the GCF is 2 × 3 = 6.

How do I find the LCM with prime factors?

Multiply every prime that appears in either one, using the larger power: 2² × 3² = 36.
