How to Multiply Fractions and Mixed Numbers

Multiplying fractions is the easy one: you do not need a common denominator. Multiply the tops, multiply the bottoms, and simplify.

Try it with your own problem ↓

The steps

  1. Turn any mixed number into an improper fraction. A whole number n becomes n/1.
  2. Optional shortcut: cancel any factor that is in a top and in a bottom.
  3. Multiply the numerators together.
  4. Multiply the denominators together.
  5. Simplify, and write the answer as a mixed number if it is improper.

A worked example

Work out 2 1/2 × 3/4.

  1. Turn 2 1/2 into an improper fraction: 2 × 2 + 1 = 5, so it is 5/2.
  2. Now we have 5/2 × 3/4. There is nothing to cancel.
  3. Multiply the tops: 5 × 3 = 15.
  4. Multiply the bottoms: 2 × 4 = 8.
  5. 15/8 is improper: 15 ÷ 8 = 1 remainder 7, so it is 1 7/8.

2 1/2 × 3/4 = 15/8 = 1 7/8. Check: 2.5 × 0.75 = 1.875. ✓

Why it works

Take half of 3/4. Cut each fourth in half and you have eighths, and you still keep 3 of them, so the answer is 3/8. The bottom doubled (4 × 2) while the top stayed at 3 (3 × 1). That is exactly "multiply across": 1/2 × 3/4 = (1 × 3)/(2 × 4).

Common mistakes

  • Looking for a common denominator. That is for adding and subtracting, not multiplying.
  • Multiplying the whole parts and fractions separately. 2 1/2 × 3/4 is not (2 × 3) and (1/2 × 4). Convert to improper first.
  • Expecting a bigger answer. Multiplying by a fraction less than 1 makes the number smaller.
  • Forgetting to simplify. Cancel early to keep the numbers small.

Questions people ask

How do I multiply a fraction by a whole number?

Write the whole number over 1: 3 × 2/5 = 3/1 × 2/5 = 6/5 = 1 1/5.

What does "of" mean in a fraction problem?

Multiply. Half of 3/4 means 1/2 × 3/4 = 3/8.

Why is the answer smaller than what I started with?

Taking a part of a part gives less than the part you started with. 1/2 of 3/4 is smaller than 3/4.
