How to Find the Prime Factorization of a Number

A prime number has exactly two factors, 1 and itself (2, 3, 5, 7, 11, …). The prime factorization of a number writes it as a product of primes, and every number above 1 has exactly one.

Try it with your own problem ↓

The steps

  1. Start with the number. If it is prime, you are done.
  2. Find any prime that divides it evenly. Try 2, then 3, then 5, then 7, and so on. Quick tests: even numbers divide by 2; digits that add to a multiple of 3 divide by 3; numbers ending in 0 or 5 divide by 5.
  3. Divide, and write that prime down. You now have a smaller number.
  4. Repeat with the smaller number until you reach 1 (or until the last number is itself prime).
  5. Write the primes as a product, using exponents for repeats.

A worked example

Find the prime factorization of 360.

  1. 360 is even: 360 ÷ 2 = 180.
  2. 180 is even: 180 ÷ 2 = 90.
  3. 90 is even: 90 ÷ 2 = 45.
  4. 45 is odd, but its digits add to 9, so 45 ÷ 3 = 15.
  5. 15 ÷ 3 = 5.
  6. 5 is prime. The primes used are 2, 2, 2, 3, 3, 5.

360 = 2 × 2 × 2 × 3 × 3 × 5 = 2³ × 3² × 5. Check: 8 × 9 × 5 = 360. ✓

Common mistakes

  • Stopping at a composite number. 360 = 6 × 60 is not finished. Keep splitting until every factor is prime.
  • Calling 1 prime. 1 is not prime, so it never appears in the factorization.
  • Missing repeats. If a prime divides more than once, count each time.
  • Skipping 2. Start with 2, since every even number has it.

Questions people ask

Is 1 a prime number?

No. A prime needs exactly two different factors, and 1 has only one.

What is a factor tree?

A drawing that splits a number into any two factors, then splits those, until all the leaves are prime. You get the same primes whichever split you start with.

What do I use prime factorization for?

Simplifying fractions, finding the GCF and LCM, and simplifying square roots.
