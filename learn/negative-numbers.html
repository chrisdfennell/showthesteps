How to Add, Subtract, Multiply and Divide Negative Numbers

A negative number is a number below zero, like −7. Picture a number line: adding moves you right, subtracting moves you left, and a negative sign flips the direction.

Try it with your own problem ↓

The steps

  1. Subtracting a negative is adding a positive. Rewrite a − (−b) as a + b.
  2. Adding numbers with the same sign: add the sizes and keep the sign. −3 + (−4) = −7.
  3. Adding numbers with different signs: subtract the smaller size from the bigger one, and keep the sign of the number with the bigger size.
  4. Multiplying or dividing: multiply or divide the sizes. Same signs give a positive answer, different signs give a negative answer.

A worked example

Work out −7 − (−2).

  1. Subtracting a negative is the same as adding a positive, so rewrite it as −7 + 2.
  2. The signs are different. The sizes are 7 and 2; the difference is 7 − 2 = 5.
  3. The bigger size (7) is negative, so the answer is negative: −5.
  4. On a number line: start at −7 and move 2 to the right. You land on −5.

−7 − (−2) = −5.

Common mistakes

  • Ignoring the second sign. In −7 − (−2), the two minus signs together mean plus.
  • Dropping the sign of the bigger number. −9 + 4 is −5, not 5.
  • Thinking two negatives always give a negative. They do when adding (−2 + −3 = −5) but not when multiplying (−2 × −3 = 6).
  • Mixing up −3² and (−3)². The first is −9. The second is 9.

Questions people ask

Why is a negative times a negative positive?

Think of reversing a reversal. Taking away a debt of $2, three times, leaves you $6 better off: −(−2) × 3 = 6. The sign rules keep all of arithmetic consistent.

Is −5 greater or less than −8?

Greater. On a number line it is farther to the right. Of two negatives, the one closer to zero is larger.

What is the absolute value of −7?

7. It is the distance from zero, so it is never negative.
