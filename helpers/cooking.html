    About the Recipe Card

    The Recipe Card works kitchen math step by step: scaling a recipe up or down with exact fractions, cups, tablespoons and teaspoons, grams to cups for common ingredients, baker’s percentages and hydration, food cost and menu prices, oven temperatures in °F, °C and gas marks, when to start a roast, swapping pan sizes, and brines and ratios. Each step says what to do and why, with the pans drawn to the same scale and the dough as a bar of percents. Amounts are US measures and ingredient weights are typical values; for doneness, use a food thermometer, not time alone.

    A worked example: an 8x8 recipe in a 9x13 pan

    1. The recipe’s pan: 8 × 8 = 64 square inches.
    2. The new pan: 13 × 9 = 117 square inches.
    3. Compare the areas, not the widths: 117 ÷ 64 = 1.8281, about 1.83, so scale every ingredient by about 1 3/4.
    4. At the same depth, bake at the same temperature and start checking a little early; a deeper pan needs longer.

    Common questions

    How do I scale a recipe to more servings?

    Divide the servings you want by the servings the recipe makes, then multiply every amount by that factor. From 4 servings to 10 the factor is 10 ÷ 4 = 2 1/2, so 1 cup becomes 2 1/2 cups.

    How many tablespoons are in a cup?

    A US cup is 16 tablespoons, so 1 1/2 cups is 3/2 × 16 = 24 tablespoons. A tablespoon is 3 teaspoons.

    What is 350 °F in Celsius?

    Subtract 32, then multiply by 5/9: 350 − 32 = 318, and 318 × 5 ÷ 9 = 176.67 °C, so set the oven to about 180 °C. Subtract first; multiplying first gives the wrong answer.
