    About the Framing Square

    The Framing Square works carpentry and construction math step by step: adding and halving feet, inches and fractions on a tape measure, board feet and lumber cost, stair risers, treads and stringers, roof pitch and rafter length, checking a corner for square with 3-4-5, stud and baluster spacing, flooring, tile, sheets and paint with waste, concrete in cubic yards and bags, and miter angles. Each step says what to do and why, and the work is drawn to scale: the stairs from the side, the rafter, the studs along the wall. The code numbers are typical values, so check your local building codes before you build.

    A worked example: stairs with a total rise of 104 inches

    1. Divide the total rise by the tallest riser allowed (7 3/4" is typical): 104 ÷ 7 3/4 = 13.42, so round up to 14 risers. With 13, each would be 8", too tall.
    2. Riser height: 104 ÷ 14 = 7 3/7", about 7 7/16" to the nearest 1/16.
    3. Total run: there is one tread fewer than risers, so 13 treads × 10" = 130", or 10' 10".
    4. Stringer: √(104² + 130²) ≈ 166.48", about 13' 10 1/2" along the slope.

    Common questions

    How do I calculate board feet?

    Multiply the thickness and width in inches by the length in feet, then divide by 12. A 2×6 that is 12 ft long is 2 × 6 × 12 ÷ 12 = 12 board feet. The count uses the nominal size: the wood itself is 1 1/2" × 5 1/2", about 69% of that.

    How many cubic yards of concrete do I need for a slab?

    Multiply length × width × thickness in feet, then divide by 27. A 20 by 24 ft slab 4 inches thick is 20 × 24 × 1/3 = 160 cubic feet, and 160 ÷ 27 ≈ 5.93, so order at least 6 cubic yards. Many crews add 5–10% for waste.

    How many studs do I need for a wall?

    Divide the wall length by the spacing, round up, and add one. A 14 ft wall is 168", and 168 ÷ 16 = 10.5, so 11 spaces and 12 studs at 16" on center. Add studs for corners, doors and windows.
