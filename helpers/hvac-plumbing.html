    About the Pipe Wrench

    The Pipe Wrench works HVAC and plumbing math step by step for trade students: BTU, watts and tons of cooling, airflow and sensible heat (1.08 × CFM × ΔT), duct velocity and size, heat loss through a wall or window, gallons in a pipe or tank, fill time and gpm, water pressure and feet of head, drain slope, and how long a water heater takes. Each step says what to do and why, and says where its constant comes from, with a drawing of the duct, the pipe or the drain. These are textbook formulas at standard conditions, not a design: sizing a real system needs a full load calculation and your local code.

    A worked example: heating 50 gallons from 50 °F to 120 °F with a 4,500 W element

    1. The temperature rise: 120 − 50 = 70°F.
    2. Heat needed: a gallon of water weighs 8.33 lb, so 50 gallons is 416.5 lb, and 416.5 × 70 = 29,155 BTU.
    3. The element: 4,500 W × 3.412 = 15,354 BTU/hr, and an electric element puts nearly all of it into the water.
    4. Time: 29,155 ÷ 15,354 ≈ 1.9 hours, about 1 hr 54 min.

    Common questions

    How many BTUs is a ton of cooling?

    One ton of cooling is 12,000 BTU per hour, so divide BTU/hr by 12,000 to get tons. A 36,000 BTU air conditioner is 36,000 ÷ 12,000 = 3 tons.

    What is the 1.08 formula for airflow?

    Sensible heat in BTU/hr = 1.08 × CFM × the temperature change in °F. The 1.08 is 0.075 lb per cubic foot of air × 0.24 BTU per pound per degree × 60 minutes. So 1,200 CFM with a 20 degree rise carries 1.08 × 1,200 × 20 = 25,920 BTU/hr.

    How do I convert feet of head to psi?

    Multiply the feet by 0.433, or go the other way with 2.31 feet per psi. 60 feet of water is 0.433 × 60 = 25.98 psi. That is static pressure: moving water loses more to friction in the pipe.
