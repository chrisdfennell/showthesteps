    About the Cash Register

    The Cash Register works retail and small-business math step by step: markup and margin, stacked discounts, making change and counting a drawer, sales tax on a receipt and a tax-included price, hours from a time card and overtime, commission, break-even, profit and loss, inventory and the reorder point, and unit prices. Each step says what to do and why, with drawings such as the price split into cost and profit and the break-even chart. It is for learning and practice: tax rules and labor laws vary by place, so check your local rules, your employer’s policy or an accountant for real payroll and taxes.

    A worked example: cost $12, price $20, markup and margin

    1. The profit: $20.00 − $12.00 = $8.00.
    2. Markup is the profit over the cost: $8.00 ÷ $12.00 ≈ 66.67%.
    3. Margin is the profit over the price: $8.00 ÷ $20.00 = 40%.
    4. Check it: cost + profit = $12.00 + $8.00 = $20.00, the price.

    Common questions

    How do I set a price for a 40% margin?

    Divide the cost by 1 minus the margin. With a cost of $45 and a 40% margin, the cost is 60% of the price, so the price is $45.00 ÷ 0.6 = $75.00. Adding 40% to the cost ($63.00) is a 40% markup, which is only a 28.57% margin.

    How do I calculate overtime pay?

    Pay the first 40 hours at the regular rate and the hours over 40 at 1.5 times it. For 46 hours at $18: 40 × $18.00 = $720.00, plus 6 hours at $27.00 = $162.00, so $882.00 gross. Some states also pay overtime for long days, so check your state’s rules.

    How do I find the break-even point?

    Divide the fixed costs by what each unit contributes, the price minus the variable cost. With $3,000 of fixed costs, a $25 price and a $10 variable cost, each unit contributes $15, so 3,000 ÷ 15 = 200 units, or $5,000 of sales.
