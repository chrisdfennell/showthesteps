    About the Carry Column

    The Carry Column works out addition step by step, the way you would on paper: adding whole numbers and decimals with carrying (also called regrouping), and adding fractions and mixed numbers with a common denominator. Add 2 to 4 whole numbers or decimals, or two fractions, and type them with symbols or in words. Each step says which column you are adding and what gets carried, or how the fractions are renamed, with a tape diagram and an area model to show it.

    A worked example: 2 3/4 + 1 5/6

    1. Turn the mixed numbers into improper fractions: 2 3/4 is 2 × 4 + 3 = 11 fourths, so 11/4, and 1 5/6 becomes 11/6.
    2. Find the least common denominator. Multiples of 4: 4, 8, 12. Multiples of 6: 6, 12. The smallest one they share is 12.
    3. Rename both fractions in twelfths: 11/4 × 3/3 = 33/12 and 11/6 × 2/2 = 22/12.
    4. Add the numerators and keep the denominator: 33 + 22 = 55, so 55/12.
    5. Turn it back into a mixed number: 55 ÷ 12 = 4 remainder 7, so the sum is 4 7/12.

    Common questions

    What does it mean to carry in addition?

    When a column adds up to 10 or more, write the ones digit in that column and carry the ten to the next column on the left. In 47 + 38 the ones make 7 + 8 = 15, so write 5 and carry 1 to the tens. Then 4 + 3 + 1 = 8, and the sum is 85.

    How do you add decimals?

    Line up the decimal points, fill any empty places with zeros, and add each column from right to left as you would with whole numbers. The point in the answer goes straight below the others: 25.40 + 3.75 = 29.15.

    Do you add the denominators when you add fractions?

    No. The denominator tells you the size of the pieces, so it stays the same; you add only the numerators, once the pieces are the same size. 1/4 + 2/4 = 3/4, not 3/8.
