    About the Rate Machine

    The Rate Machine works out calculus step by step: limits, including what to do when plugging in gives 0/0, derivatives with the power, product, quotient and chain rules, tangent lines, maximum and minimum points, integrals and the area under a curve, rates of change and motion, and word problems on optimization and related rates. Each step names the rule it uses and why, and a graph shows the function with its slope, tangent line or shaded area.

    A worked example: the derivative of (2x + 1)⁵

    1. It is a power with something inside, so use the chain rule: bring down the 5, keep the inside, and multiply by the inside’s derivative: f′(x) = 5(2x + 1)⁴ · (2x + 1)′.
    2. The derivative of the inside is (2x + 1)′ = 2 + 0 = 2.
    3. Put it together: f′(x) = 5(2x + 1)⁴ · 2 = 10(2x + 1)⁴.

    Common questions

    What do I do when a limit gives 0/0?

    0/0 isn’t an answer; it is a signal to simplify first. For the limit of (x² − 9)/(x − 3) as x goes to 3, factor the top into (x − 3)(x + 3), cancel the x − 3, and plug in: 3 + 3 = 6.

    Why does an integral have + C?

    A constant has derivative 0, so x³ + x² + 5 and x³ + x² − 2 both have derivative 3x² + 2x. The integral of 3x² + 2x is x³ + x² + C, and the + C stands for every one of them.

    How do I find the area under a curve?

    Find an antiderivative and subtract its values at the two ends. For x² from 0 to 2, an antiderivative is x³/3, so the area is 8/3 − 0 = 8/3.
