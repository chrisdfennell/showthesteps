    About the Log Ladder

    The Log Ladder works with logarithms step by step: what a log means and how to switch between log form and exponent form, the log laws for expanding and condensing, change of base, exponential equations, log equations, growth and decay (compound interest, half-life), and the graphs of logs and exponentials. Each step says what to do and why, with a ladder of powers to show what a log is asking and pictures of the graphs.

    A worked example: log₂(x + 3) = 5

    1. A log needs a positive inside, so any answer has to make x + 3 greater than 0.
    2. Rewrite it as a power: log₂(x + 3) = 5 means x + 3 = 2⁵ = 32.
    3. Solve for x: x = 32 − 3 = 29.
    4. Check: at x = 29 the inside is 32, which is positive, and log₂ 32 = 5, so x = 29 works.

    Common questions

    What does a logarithm mean?

    A log asks for an exponent. log₂ 32 means “2 to what power gives 32?” Since 2⁵ = 32, log₂ 32 = 5.

    How do I find a log with a base my calculator doesn’t have?

    Use change of base and divide two natural logs: log₇ 50 = ln 50 ÷ ln 7 ≈ 3.912023 ÷ 1.945910 ≈ 2.0104. Divide the two logs; don’t take the log of 50 ÷ 7. As a sense check, 7² < 50 < 7³, so the answer is between 2 and 3.

    How much is $1000 worth at 5% compounded monthly for 10 years?

    Use A = P(1 + r/n)^(nt) with r = 0.05, n = 12 and t = 10: A = 1000(1 + 0.05/12)^120 ≈ $1,647.01. Simple interest would give $1,500, a little less.
