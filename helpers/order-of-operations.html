    About the Order Stack

    The Order Stack works out order of operations problems step by step, one operation per line: parentheses (and brackets) first, then exponents, then × and ÷ from left to right, then + and − from left to right. That is the rule called PEMDAS, the same as BODMAS and BEDMAS. Type the problem with symbols or in words, like “what is 6 plus 4 times 2?”, with whole numbers, decimals or fractions. Each step says which operation comes next and why, with an expression tree to show the order.

    A worked example: 20 − 12 ÷ 4 × 2 + 1

    1. × and ÷ come before + and −. Of the × and ÷, the ÷ is first from the left: 12 ÷ 4 = 3.
    2. Then the ×: 3 × 2 = 6. Now the problem is 20 − 6 + 1.
    3. Only + and − are left, so go left to right: 20 − 6 = 14.
    4. Last, 14 + 1 = 15. Going straight left to right through the whole problem would give 5 instead of 15.

    Common questions

    What is 3 + 4 × 5?

    23. Multiplication comes before addition, so 4 × 5 = 20 first, then 3 + 20 = 23. Working straight left to right would give 35, which is wrong.

    Does multiplication always come before division?

    No. × and ÷ are partners: whichever comes first, reading left to right, goes first. The same is true of + and −. So 12 ÷ 4 × 3 = 3 × 3 = 9, and 10 − 3 + 2 = 7 + 2 = 9, not 5.

    What do PEMDAS, BODMAS and BEDMAS stand for?

    They are three names for the same order: Parentheses (or Brackets), Exponents (or Orders), Multiplication and Division, then Addition and Subtraction. For example, 2 × (3 + 5)2 = 2 × 82 = 2 × 64 = 128.
