    About the Number Staircase

    The Number Staircase works out number patterns, sequences and series step by step. It finds what comes next in a pattern, the nth term of an arithmetic or geometric sequence, whether a number belongs to a sequence, and the sum of a series, even an infinite one or one written in sigma notation. It also sets up word problems about seats in rows, savings, bouncing balls, raises and doubling. Each step says what to do and why, and a graph of the terms shows the pattern.

    A worked example: the sum of the first 50 terms of 3, 7, 11, …

    1. It is arithmetic: it adds 4 each time, starting at 3.
    2. Find the last term: a₅₀ = 3 + 49 × 4 = 199.
    3. Write the sum forwards and backwards. Each column adds up to 3 + 199 = 202, and there are 50 columns, so 2S = 50 × 202.
    4. Halve it: S₅₀ = 50 × 202 ÷ 2 = 5050.

    Common questions

    How do I find any term of an arithmetic sequence?

    Start at the first term and add the common difference n − 1 times: aₙ = a₁ + (n − 1)d. In 3, 7, 11, … the 20th term is 3 + 19 × 4 = 79, not 3 + 20 × 4.

    How can I tell whether a sequence is arithmetic or geometric?

    Subtract each term from the next: if the difference is always the same, it is arithmetic. If not, divide: if the ratio is always the same, it is geometric. In 2, 6, 18, 54 the differences are 4, 12 and 36, but each term is 3 times the one before, so it is geometric and the next term is 162.

    How can an infinite series add up to a number?

    When the ratio is between −1 and 1, the terms keep shrinking and the running totals settle on a₁ ÷ (1 − r). In 12 + 6 + 3 + … the ratio is 1/2, so the sum is 12 ÷ (1 − 1/2) = 24.
