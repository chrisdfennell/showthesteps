    About the Division House

    The Division House works out long division step by step, the way it is written on paper: divide, multiply, subtract and bring down, then do it again. It divides whole numbers and decimals, with the answer as a quotient and remainder, a fraction or a decimal, and it divides fractions and mixed numbers by keep, change, flip. Each step is written out, with a tape diagram and an area model to show it, and the answer is checked by multiplying back.

    A worked example: 847 ÷ 12

    1. How many 12s fit into 84? 7. Write the 7 above the tens place.
    2. Multiply and subtract: 7 × 12 = 84, and 84 − 84 = 0.
    3. Bring down the 7. 12 doesn’t fit into 7 even once, so write 0 above the ones place.
    4. Nothing is left to bring down, so 7 is the remainder: 847 ÷ 12 = 70 R 7. Check: 70 × 12 = 840, and 840 + 7 = 847.

    Common questions

    How do you write a remainder as a fraction or a decimal?

    For a fraction, put the remainder over the divisor: 847 ÷ 12 = 70 R 7, which is 70 7/12. For a decimal, keep dividing past the decimal point by bringing down zeros: 847 ÷ 12 is about 70.583.

    How do you divide by a decimal?

    Move the decimal point in the divisor until it is a whole number, and move the point in the dividend the same number of places. 12.5 ÷ 0.4 becomes 125 ÷ 4, which is 31.25.

    How do you divide fractions?

    Keep the first fraction, change ÷ to ×, and flip the second fraction upside down. 3/4 ÷ 1/2 becomes 3/4 × 2/1 = 6/4, which is 1 1/2.
