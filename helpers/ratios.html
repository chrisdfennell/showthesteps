    About the Ratio Table

    The Ratio Table works out ratio problems step by step, the way you would on paper: simplifying ratios and finding equivalent ratios, unit rates and the better buy, solving proportions, sharing an amount in a ratio, and map scales and recipes. Type the problem with numbers, like 12:18 or 3/4 = x/20, or in words, like “6 apples for $4.50”. Each step says what to do and why, with a ratio table, a double number line or a bar model to show it.

    A worked example: share 60 in the ratio 2:3

    1. The ratio 2:3 means that for every 2 that go to the first part, 3 go to the second. Picture equal boxes (units): the first part gets 2 boxes and the second part gets 3.
    2. Count all the boxes: 2 + 3 = 5 units, and together they make the whole 60.
    3. Share it out equally: 60 ÷ 5 = 12, so each unit is worth 12.
    4. The first part is 2 × 12 = 24 and the second part is 3 × 12 = 36. They add back up to 60.

    Common questions

    How do you find a unit rate?

    Divide both amounts by the number of units, so you get the amount for one. 6 apples for $4.50 is $4.50 ÷ 6 = $0.75 per apple.

    How do you work out the better buy?

    Find the unit price of each offer and pick the lower one. 12 oz for $3.60 is $0.30 per oz, and 16 oz for $4.40 is $0.275, about $0.28, per oz. So the 16 oz offer is the better buy, even though it costs more in all.

    How do you solve a proportion?

    Cross-multiply, then divide. For 3/4 = x/20: 3 × 20 = 4 × x, so 60 = 4x and x = 60 ÷ 4 = 15. A ratio table shows the same thing: 4 × 5 = 20, so 3 × 5 = 15.
