    About the Graph Paper

    The Graph Paper works on the coordinate plane step by step: plotting points and finding the distance and midpoint, filling a table of values, graphing a function and finding its key features (vertex, intercepts), solving systems of equations and inequalities by graphing, linear programming (optimization), moving shapes with translations, reflections, rotations and dilations, and fitting a trend line to a scatter plot. Each step says what to do and why, and everything is drawn on the grid as you go.

    A worked example: x + y = 6 and x − y = 2 by graphing

    1. Get y by itself in each equation: x + y = 6 becomes y = −x + 6 (slope −1, y-intercept 6), and x − y = 2 becomes y = x − 2 (slope 1, y-intercept −2).
    2. Graph each line: start at its y-intercept and use the slope as rise over run to find more points.
    3. The slopes are different, so the lines cross exactly once. Read the crossing point off the grid: (4, 2).
    4. Check it in both original equations: 4 + 2 = 6 and 4 − 2 = 2.

    Common questions

    How do I find the distance between two points?

    Make a right triangle and use the Pythagorean theorem. From (1, 2) to (4, 6) the legs are 4 − 1 = 3 across and 6 − 2 = 4 up, so the distance is √(3² + 4²) = √25 = 5.

    How do I know which side to shade when I graph an inequality?

    Graph the boundary line, then test a point such as (0, 0). For y < 2x + 1, 0 < 2(0) + 1 is true, so shade the side with (0, 0) in it, below the line. The line is dashed because < doesn’t include the points on it.

    How do I find the vertex of a parabola?

    For y = ax² + bx + c the axis of symmetry is x = −b ÷ 2a. In y = x² − 4x + 3 that is x = 4 ÷ 2 = 2, and y = 2² − 4(2) + 3 = −1, so the vertex is (2, −1).
