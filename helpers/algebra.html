    About the Balance Scale

    The Balance Scale solves algebra step by step: it simplifies expressions, solves linear equations and inequalities (compound and absolute value inequalities too), solves a system of two equations by elimination or substitution, and solves a formula for one letter. Each move says what is done to both sides and why, and the page draws the equation as a balance scale, the answer to an inequality on a number line, or the two lines of a system crossing on a graph.

    A worked example: 5x − 4 = 2x + 8

    1. Get the x’s on one side: subtract 2x from both sides, which leaves 3x − 4 = 8.
    2. Undo the − 4: add 4 to both sides, so 3x = 12.
    3. Undo the times 3: divide both sides by 3, so x = 4.
    4. Check in the original: 5(4) − 4 = 16 and 2(4) + 8 = 16, so the two sides match.

    Common questions

    Why does the inequality sign flip when you divide by a negative number?

    Multiplying or dividing by a negative number swaps which side is bigger: 2 < 3, but −2 > −3. So in −2x + 5 > 11, subtracting 5 gives −2x > 6, and dividing by −2 turns the sign around: x < −3.

    How do I check my answer to an equation?

    Put the answer back into the original equation and work out each side. For 5x − 4 = 2x + 8 and x = 4, the left side is 16 and the right side is 16, so x = 4 is right.

    How do I solve a system of two equations?

    Get rid of one letter, solve for the other, then put it back. In 2x + y = 7 and x − y = 2, adding the equations cancels y: 3x = 9, so x = 3. Then 3 − y = 2 gives y = 1, so the answer is (3, 1), the point where the two lines cross.
