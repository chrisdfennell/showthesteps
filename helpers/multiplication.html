    About the Product Ladder

    The Product Ladder works out multiplication step by step, the way you would on paper: long multiplication of whole numbers and decimals, with one rung (a partial product) for each digit, and multiplying fractions and mixed numbers straight across. Type the problem with ×, x or *, or in words, like “3 groups of 4”. Each step says what to multiply, what gets carried and where the decimal point goes, with a tape diagram and an area model to show it, and the answer is checked by dividing back.

    A worked example: 2.4 × 1.3

    1. Set the decimal points aside for now: 2.4 × 1.3 becomes 24 × 13.
    2. First rung, the 3: 3 × 4 = 12, so write 2 and carry 1; 3 × 2 = 6, plus the 1 carried makes 7. That gives 72.
    3. Second rung, the 1: it is in the tens place, so 1 × 24 = 24 gets a zero on the end: 240.
    4. Add the rungs: 72 + 240 = 312.
    5. 2.4 and 1.3 have 1 decimal place each, 2 in all, so count 2 places in from the right of 312: the product is 3.12.

    Common questions

    How do you multiply two-digit numbers?

    Multiply the top number by each digit of the bottom number, one row at a time, and put a zero on the end of the row for the tens digit. For 47 × 38: 8 × 47 = 376 and 30 × 47 = 1,410, and 376 + 1,410 = 1,786.

    Where does the decimal point go when you multiply decimals?

    Multiply as if there were no points, then count the decimal places in both numbers together and give the answer that many. For 0.25 × 0.4, 25 × 4 = 100, and there are 3 decimal places in all, so the answer is 0.100, which is 0.1.

    How do you multiply fractions and mixed numbers?

    Multiply the tops, then multiply the bottoms; you don’t need a common denominator. Turn any mixed number into an improper fraction first: 2 1/2 × 3/4 = 5/2 × 3/4 = 15/8, which is 1 7/8.
