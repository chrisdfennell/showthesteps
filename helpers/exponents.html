    About the Power Tower

    The Power Tower works out exponent problems step by step: working out a power as repeated multiplication, zero and negative exponents too, simplifying with the exponent rules (laws of exponents), writing numbers in scientific notation, multiplying and dividing in scientific notation, and finding square and cube roots. Every number is worked out exactly. Each step names the rule it uses and why it works, with a table of powers or the jumps of the decimal point to show it.

    A worked example: 45,000,000 in scientific notation

    1. Scientific notation is a number from 1 up to (but not including) 10, times a power of 10.
    2. Move the decimal point to just after the first digit that isn’t 0: 45,000,000 becomes 4.5. That is 7 jumps to the left.
    3. Moving the point 7 jumps to the left divided by 107, so put back × 107: 4.5 × 107.
    4. Check the sign: a big number (10 or more) has a positive power. And 4.5 × 10,000,000 = 45,000,000.

    Common questions

    Is −32 the same as (−3)2?

    No. In −32 the exponent is on the 3 only, so it is the opposite of 3 × 3: −9. In (−3)2 the brackets make −3 the base: (−3) × (−3) = 9.

    Why is a number to the power 0 equal to 1?

    Go down a table of powers: each one is the one above it divided by the base. 52 = 25, 51 = 5, and 5 ÷ 5 = 1, so 50 = 1. One more step gives 5−1 = 1/5, which is why a negative exponent means one over the power. This works for any base except 0.

    How do you multiply numbers in scientific notation?

    Multiply the front numbers and add the powers of 10. (3 × 104)(2 × 105) = (3 × 2) × 104 + 5 = 6 × 109, which is 6,000,000,000.
