    About the Gear Box

    The Gear Box works auto shop math step by step: gear ratios, road speed from engine rpm (and the rpm at a speed), tire sizes and speedometer error, engine displacement, compression ratio, horsepower and torque, fuel economy and trip cost, 2-stroke oil and coolant mixtures, and the shop invoice with labor, parts markup and tax. Each step says what to do and why, with drawings such as the tire from the side and the wheel rolling one turn. The numbers are worked examples: fluid types, capacities, mix ratios and tire sizes come from the manufacturer’s specs and the service manual.

    A worked example: the diameter of a P225/60R16 tire

    1. Read the size: 225 mm wide, an aspect ratio of 60%, on a 16 inch rim.
    2. Sidewall: 225 × 60% = 135 mm, and 135 ÷ 25.4 = 5.31 inches.
    3. Diameter: two sidewalls plus the rim, 2 × 5.31 + 16 = 26.63 inches tall.
    4. Revolutions per mile: one turn is π × 26.63 = 83.66 inches, and 63,360 ÷ 83.66 = 757 turns a mile.

    Common questions

    How do I calculate horsepower from torque?

    Multiply the torque in lb-ft by the rpm and divide by 5,252: hp = lb-ft × rpm ÷ 5,252. 300 lb-ft at 5,000 rpm is 300 × 5,000 ÷ 5,252 = 285.6 hp. Below 5,252 rpm the horsepower number is smaller than the torque number.

    How much 2-stroke oil do I mix for 50:1?

    Divide the gas by 50. A gallon is 128 fl oz, so 128 ÷ 50 = 2.56 fl oz of oil, about 76 mL. Use the oil and the ratio in the owner’s manual.

    How do bigger tires change the speedometer?

    Divide the new diameter by the old one. A P225/60R16 is 26.63" tall and a P245/55R17 is 27.61", and 27.61 ÷ 26.63 ≈ 1.0368, so when the speedometer shows 60 mph you are really going 62.2 mph. It reads 3.68% low.
