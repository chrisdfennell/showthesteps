    About the Slope Grid

    The Slope Grid works with straight lines on the coordinate grid: the slope of a line through two points (rise over run), the equation of a line from a slope and a point or from two points, converting between slope-intercept, point-slope and standard form, graphing a line, and parallel and perpendicular lines. Each step explains what to do and why, and the line is drawn on a grid with its rise-and-run staircase.

    A worked example: the slope through (1, 2) and (5, 4)

    1. Call (1, 2) the first point and (5, 4) the second. Either order works, as long as you subtract in the same order.
    2. The rise is the change in y: 4 − 2 = 2.
    3. The run is the change in x: 5 − 1 = 4.
    4. Slope is rise over run: m = 2/4, and dividing the top and bottom by 2 gives m = 1/2.

    Common questions

    How do I find the slope from two points?

    Subtract the y-values to get the rise and the x-values to get the run, in the same order, then divide. From (1, 2) to (5, 4) the rise is 4 − 2 = 2 and the run is 5 − 1 = 4, so the slope is 2/4 = 1/2.

    How do I write the equation of a line from a slope and a point?

    Use point-slope form, y − y1 = m(x − x1), then get y by itself. With slope 2 through (1, 3): y − 3 = 2(x − 1), so y − 3 = 2x − 2 and y = 2x + 1.

    How can I tell if two lines are parallel or perpendicular?

    Compare their slopes. Parallel lines have the same slope; perpendicular lines have slopes that multiply to −1. y = 2x + 1 and y = −1/2x + 3 are perpendicular, because 2 · (−1/2) = −1.
