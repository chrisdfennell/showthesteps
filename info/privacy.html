Privacy

Your work stays with you.

No accounts, no ads and no cookies. Here is exactly what Show The Steps keeps, where, and what (little) is sent anywhere.

The short version: every problem is worked out in your own browser, and nothing you type is sent to anyone, unless you press Send this problem on a problem a page couldn't read. Your progress and settings are saved only on your device. The site counts visits anonymously, without cookies. It collects no personal information from anyone, including children, so there is no student data to share or sell.

What stays on your device

The site saves a few things in your browser's local storage, so they're still there next time. They never leave the device:

WhatWhy
SettingsLight or dark theme, text size, the read-aloud voice and speed, and whether the math keypad shows.
Each helper's last problemSo a page opens where you left it.
Recent problemsThe Recent list at the top of each page.
Your progressWhat you've practiced and which mistakes to revisit, shown on Your progress.
Problem of the dayYour streak, and which days you've done.
Flash cards, practice sets, worksheetsHow well you know each card, a practice set in progress, and the worksheet maker's choices.
Class resultsOnly if a teacher chooses to keep pasted results on their own device.

To delete it: Your progress has a Clear my progress button, and clearing this site's data in your browser's settings removes everything. If you install the site as an app, your browser also keeps a copy of the pages so they work offline; removing the app or clearing site data deletes that too.

What is sent, and to whom

ServiceWhat it gets
GoatCounter
visit counts		 Each page view: the page's address without the problem in it (so what you typed is never included), the site you came from, your screen size, and your browser and operating system. GoatCounter works out the country from your IP address and then discards the address. When a page can't read a problem you typed, it also counts that, with only the page's name (not what you typed), so we can see which pages need to understand more. No cookies, and nothing that identifies you.
GoatCounter
a problem you send		 Only if you press Send this problem so we can teach the page (it appears under a "couldn't read that" message): that one problem, as you typed it, and the page's name, so the page can be taught to read problems like it. Email addresses are taken out and it's cut to 300 characters; nothing else is sent with it, and nothing is sent unless you press the button. Please leave out names and anything personal. GoatCounter's privacy policy.
GitHub Pages
the hosting		 The site is served by GitHub, which may log visitors' IP addresses for security. GitHub's privacy statement.
jsDelivr
the picture reader		 Only if you use the camera button to read a problem from a photo: the first time, your browser downloads the reading engine (Tesseract.js and its English reading data, about 7 MB) from jsDelivr, which sees your IP address like any download. Your browser keeps it, so later pictures are read without downloading it again. Your picture is never sent. jsDelivr's privacy policy.

Nothing else is sent. In particular:

  • Photos of a problem (the camera button by the problem box, or a picture you paste or drop in) are read on your device and never uploaded or saved: only the words and numbers read from it are put in the box.
  • Read aloud uses the voices built into your device or browser. Some browsers offer online voices (with names like "Online" or "Google"); if you pick one of those, your browser may send the text being read to its maker's speech service. The other voices work entirely on your device.
  • Practice sets and class results are never uploaded: a student copies their result line and hands it to the teacher, who pastes it in.
  • QR codes are drawn on your device, and the problem of the day is worked out from the date, on your device.
  • Helpers embedded on another website (a class page, Google Sites, Canvas) work the same way: what's typed stays in the browser, and embedded pages aren't counted in the visit numbers. A helper may remember its last problem in the visitor's browser; browsers keep that apart for each website it's embedded in.
  • The fonts come from the site itself, so your browser doesn't contact Google or any other font server.
  • There are no accounts, logins, ads, trackers or cookies.
